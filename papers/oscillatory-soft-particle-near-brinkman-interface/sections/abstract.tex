\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Context:} colloidal probes, cells and vesicles oscillate (AFM, acoustofluidics) next to permeable
        biological layers such as mucus; the frequency-dependent force carries information on both the particle and the
        layer.
  \item \textbf{Gap:} the unsteady force on a rigid sphere near a rigid wall is known, and a homogeneous porous sphere near a
        Brinkman half-space has just been treated; the sign structure of the wall-induced added mass near a permeable
        layer, and the role of particle softness, are not known.
  \item \textbf{What we do:} a semi-analytical solution of the unsteady Stokes--Brinkman problem for a core--shell
        particle oscillating normal or parallel to a Brinkman half-space with stress jump, combining the exact particle
        response, an exact Fourier reflection and a self-consistent dipolar closure (error $5\%$ normal, $1.5\%$
        parallel at $\varepsilon=1$, converging as $x^4$); positive stress jumps act as interfacial energy sources, which
        restricts the admissible range.
  \item \textbf{Key results:} (i) a permeable layer becomes hydrodynamically transparent above a gap-independent
        crossover $\Wo_c^{\downarrow}=\alpha_p^2/(6\sqrt2)\approx0.118\,\alpha_p^2$, derived asymptotically and confirmed
        numerically to $1.5\%$; (ii) the negative wall-induced added mass at low frequency is the Basset memory force
        removed by the wall, tending to the full Basset term; (iii) permeability raises the lower crossover
        ($\Wo_c\approx2.9$ normal, $0.95$ parallel near a rigid wall) and closes the positive-added-mass window below
        $\alpha_p\approx6$--$9$ (normal), $4$--$6$ (parallel); (iv) particle softness suppresses the near-wall damping
        approximately as the square of the particle's drag ratio.
  \item \textbf{Implication:} a frequency sweep of the near-wall force could measure layer permeability without knowing
        the probe--wall distance (kHz--MHz for $10\,\mu$m probes near mucus-like layers) and distinguishes particles of
        different softness, relevant to colloidal-probe microrheology, acoustofluidic cell sorting and
        mucus-penetrating carriers.
\end{itemize}
