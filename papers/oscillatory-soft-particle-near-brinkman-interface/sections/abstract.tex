\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Context:} colloidal probes, cells and vesicles oscillate (AFM, acoustofluidics) next to permeable
        biological layers such as the endothelial glycocalyx and mucus; the frequency-dependent force carries information
        on both the particle and the layer.
  \item \textbf{Gap:} the unsteady force has been resolved only for a rigid sphere near a rigid wall; the porous layer
        (Brinkman, Ochoa-Tapia--Whitaker stress jump) and the soft (permeable-shell) particle have not been treated.
  \item \textbf{What we do:} a semi-analytical solution of the unsteady Stokes--Brinkman problem for a core--shell
        particle oscillating normal or parallel to a Brinkman half-space, combining the exact particle response, an exact
        Fourier reflection and a self-consistent dipolar closure; validated against exact, steady and inviscid limits
        (error $5\%$ normal, $1.5\%$ parallel at $\varepsilon=1$).
  \item \textbf{Key results:} near a rigid wall the wall-induced added mass changes sign at $\Wo_c\approx2.9$ (normal) and
        $0.95$ (parallel); its low-frequency negative part is the Basset memory force removed by the wall. A permeable
        layer raises the lower crossover, adds a gap-independent upper crossover $\Wo_c^{\downarrow}\approx0.116\,\alpha_p^2$
        above which it becomes transparent, and below a threshold ($\alpha_p\approx6$--$9$ normal, $4$--$6$ parallel)
        removes the positive-added-mass window. Soft shells reduce the near-wall damping approximately as the square of
        the particle's drag ratio and shift the crossovers non-monotonically.
  \item \textbf{Implication:} a frequency sweep of the near-wall force could provide a non-contact, gap-independent
        measure of layer permeability and of particle softness, relevant to glycocalyx microrheology, acoustofluidic CTC
        sorting and mucus-penetrating carriers.
\end{itemize}
