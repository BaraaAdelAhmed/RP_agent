\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% GUIDE BULLETS -- the authors turn these into prose.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Summary:} semi-analytical theory for the oscillatory force on a soft core--shell particle near a
        Brinkman half-space with stress jump, built from the exact dipolar particle response, an exact Fourier reflection
        and a self-consistent dipolar closure; validated against exact, steady and inviscid limits, with an error of
        $5\%$ (normal) and $1.5\%$ (parallel) at $\varepsilon=1$ that decreases rapidly with the gap.
  \item \textbf{Rigid reference:} near a rigid wall the wall-induced added mass changes sign once, at $\Wo_c\approx2.9$
        (normal) and $0.95$ (parallel) for $\varepsilon=1$, consistent qualitatively with the AFM frequency-shift crossover
        of Ref.~\onlinecite{zhang2023unsteady}. Its negative low-frequency part is the Basset memory force removed by the
        wall and tends to the full Basset term as $\Wo\to0$.
  \item \textbf{Permeable layer:} a finite $\alpha_p$ raises the lower crossover, adds an upper crossover
        $\Wo_c^{\downarrow}\approx0.116\,\alpha_p^2$ that is independent of the gap and marks the frequency above which the
        layer becomes transparent, and closes the positive-added-mass window below $\alpha_p\approx6$--$9$ (normal) and
        $4$--$6$ (parallel). The damping has a maximum at $\Wo/\alpha_p\approx1$--$2$, up to twice the rigid-wall value.
        The stress jump $\beta_p$ shifts these boundaries only weakly; the steady drag changes by a few per cent.
  \item \textbf{Soft particle:} permeable shells reduce the near-wall damping approximately as $\Omega^2$; homogeneous
        porous particles ($\kappa=0$, $\alpha_s\le8$) lose the crossover in normal oscillation, and intermediate cores
        produce non-monotonic and re-entrant crossovers. Surface slip matters for impermeable particles ($-42\%$ damping
        for $\hat\ell_c=0.5$), the shell stress jump for soft ones ($-31\%$ to $+17\%$ for $\beta_s=\pm0.5$).
  \item \textbf{Biomedical takeaways:} (i) the crossover frequencies could serve as a non-contact, gap-independent
        fingerprint of layer permeability (glycocalyx degradation, mucus hydration), e.g.\ in the kHz--MHz range for
        $10\,\mu$m probes; (ii) cell-like particles of different softness differ in near-wall force amplitude (factor
        $1.4$--$2.7$) and phase (up to $13^\circ$), most usefully at $\Wo=O(1)$ for acoustofluidic sorting; (iii) soft,
        permeable shells reduce near-wall resistance, which supports their use in mucus-penetrating carriers.
  \item \textbf{Limitations:} dipolar closure valid for $\varepsilon\gtrsim1$ (no lubrication); rigid stationary porous
        matrices; no particle rotation; layer modelled as a half-space; Brinkman viscosities equal to $\mu$.
  \item \textbf{Future work:} asymptotic theory of the transparency law $\Wo_c^{\downarrow}\propto\alpha_p^2$; full
        multipole collocation for $\varepsilon<1$; finite-thickness and poroelastic layers; free rotation; comparison with
        colloidal-probe AFM on glycocalyx-mimicking brushes.
\end{itemize}
