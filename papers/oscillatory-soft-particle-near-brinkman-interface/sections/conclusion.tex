\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% GUIDE BULLETS -- the authors turn these into prose.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Transparency law: a permeable layer becomes hydrodynamically transparent above
        $\Wo_c^{\downarrow}=\alpha_p^2/(6\sqrt2)\approx0.118\,\alpha_p^2$ ($\omega\approx\mu b^2/(72\rho k_p^2)$), independent
        of the gap and, at leading order, of the direction of oscillation; derived asymptotically and confirmed by the
        full computation to within $1.5\%$.
  \item Memory removal: near a wall the wall-induced added mass is negative at low frequency because the wall
        removes the Basset memory force; $-\hat\chi_{\rm int}$ tends to the full Basset term $\Wo/\sqrt2$ (ratio $0.99$ normal,
        $0.96$ parallel at $\Wo=0.01$). This explains, qualitatively, the frequency-shift crossover observed with
        colloidal-probe AFM by Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2023unsteady}.
  \item Crossover map: near a rigid wall the added mass changes sign once ($\Wo_c\approx2.9$ normal, $0.95$
        parallel at $\varepsilon=1$); permeability raises this crossover, closes the positive-added-mass window below
        $\alpha_p\approx6$--$9$ (normal) and $4$--$6$ (parallel), and splits it in two in parallel oscillation near the
        threshold. The damping peaks at $\Wo/\alpha_p\approx1$--$2$; the steady drag changes by only a few per cent.
  \item Soft particles: permeable shells reduce the near-wall damping approximately as $\Omega^2$ and reshape the
        crossovers; slip matters for impermeable particles ($-42\%$ damping for $\hat\ell_c=0.5$), the shell stress jump
        for soft ones ($-31\%$ to $+17\%$ for $\beta_s=\pm0.5$); cell-like particles of different softness differ in
        near-wall force by a factor $1.4$--$2.7$ and in phase by up to $13^\circ$.
  \item Method and admissibility: exact dipolar particle response, exact Fourier reflection and a
        self-consistent closure, accurate to $5\%$ (normal) and $1.5\%$ (parallel) at $\varepsilon=1$; the energy identity
        shows that positive stress-jump coefficients act as interfacial energy sources, restricting $\beta_s,\beta_p\le0.5$.
  \item Applications: gap-independent, non-contact estimation of the permeability of mucus-like layers
        (kHz--MHz for $10\,\mu$m probes); for the much denser glycocalyx, micrometre particles see an almost rigid wall.
  \item Limitations and future work: dipolar closure for $\varepsilon\gtrsim1$ (no lubrication); rigid
        stationary porous matrices; no rotation; half-space layer; Brinkman viscosities equal to $\mu$. Next: multipole
        collocation for $\varepsilon<1$, finite-thickness and poroelastic layers, free rotation, comparison with
        colloidal-probe AFM on hydrogel layers.
\end{itemize}
