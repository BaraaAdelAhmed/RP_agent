\section{Linear system for the core--shell particle}\label{app:sphere}
This appendix lists everything needed to assemble the particle response \eqref{eq:response}: the radial kernels and
their derivatives, the amplitudes of each term of \cref{eq:fields}, and the six boundary conditions.

The kernels of \cref{sec:sphere} and their derivatives, which enter $\mathcal U$, $\mathcal V$ and their radial
derivatives through \cref{eq:vort_radial}, are
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal K_j'&=-\frac{(1+\lambda_jr)\,e^{-\lambda_jr}}{r^2}, &
  \mathcal I_j'&=\frac{\lambda_jr\cosh\lambda_jr-\sinh\lambda_jr}{r^2},\\
  \mathcal K_j''&=\frac{(2+2\lambda_jr+\lambda_j^2r^2)\,e^{-\lambda_jr}}{r^3}, &
  \mathcal I_j''&=\frac{(\lambda_j^2r^2+2)\sinh\lambda_jr-2\lambda_jr\cosh\lambda_jr}{r^3},
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
  \mathcal K_j'''&=-\frac{(6+6\lambda_jr+3\lambda_j^2r^2+\lambda_j^3r^3)\,e^{-\lambda_jr}}{r^4},\\
  \mathcal I_j'''&=\frac{(\lambda_j^3r^3+6\lambda_jr)\cosh\lambda_jr-(3\lambda_j^2r^2+6)\sinh\lambda_jr}{r^4}.
\end{align*}
For a vortical term, $\mathcal U=g''-\lambda_j^2g$, $\mathcal V=\lambda_j^2g-g'/r$, $\mathcal U'=g'''-\lambda_j^2g'$ and
$\mathcal V'=\lambda_j^2g'-g''/r+g'/r^2$, with $\mathcal P=0$. For the potential terms, with the pressure
coefficients $c_j$ of \cref{lem:decomp},
\begin{align*}
  \be:&\quad \mathcal U=1,\ \mathcal V=-1,\ \mathcal P=c_j\,r,\\
  \nabla(\be\cdot\nabla r^{-1}):&\quad \mathcal U=2r^{-3},\ \mathcal V=r^{-3},\ \mathcal P=-c_j\,r^{-2}.
\end{align*}
The rigid part $\be$ of \cref{eq:field_shell} carries the pressure $i\Wo^2\be\cdot\bx$, so its amplitudes at $r=1$ are
$\mathcal U=1$, $\mathcal V=-1$, $\mathcal S_{rr}=-i\Wo^2$ and $\mathcal S_{r\theta}=0$. A tilde denotes the shell
field without its rigid part, and shell tractions are computed with $\mu_s/\mu$. The core conditions
\eqref{eq:bc_core} at $r=\kappa$ are
\begin{equation*}
  \tilde{\mathcal U}^{(2)}=0,\qquad \tilde{\mathcal V}^{(2)}-\frac{\hat\ell_c}{\mu_s/\mu}\,\tilde{\mathcal S}^{(2)}_{r\theta}=0,
\end{equation*}
and the shell conditions \eqref{eq:bc_shell} at $r=1$ are
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal U^{(1)}=\mathcal U^{(2)},\qquad \mathcal V^{(1)}=\mathcal V^{(2)},\qquad
  \mathcal S^{(1)}_{rr}=\mathcal S^{(2)}_{rr},\qquad
  \mathcal S^{(2)}_{r\theta}-\mathcal S^{(1)}_{r\theta}=\beta_s\alpha_s\bigl(\mathcal V^{(1)}+1\bigr).
\end{equation*}
These six equations are linear in $(B,C,E_1,E_2,G_1,G_2)$, with right-hand sides proportional to $U$, $U_p$ and
$w_0$. Solving them for the three unit inputs gives the columns of $\bm s_U$ and $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}$, and
\cref{eq:force_amplitudes} gives $Z_U$, $Z_p$ and $Z_w$. To avoid overflow when $|\lambda_s|$ or $|\lambda|$ is large,
the kernels are used in the rescaled forms $\mathcal K_s(r)e^{\lambda_s\kappa}$, $\mathcal I_s(r)e^{-\lambda_s}$,
$\mathcal K_f(r)e^{\lambda}$ and $\mathcal I_f(r)e^{-\lambda}$, and the scale factors are removed from the coefficients
after the solve. For a homogeneous porous particle ($\kappa=0$, $\beta_s=0$, $\mu_s=\mu$, $\Wo\to0$) the solver
reproduces the Debye--Bueche--Brinkman drag ratio
$\Omega=2\alpha_s^2(1-\alpha_s^{-1}\tanh\alpha_s)/[2\alpha_s^2+3(1-\alpha_s^{-1}\tanh\alpha_s)]$
(\cref{tab:validation}).

\section{Interface system in Fourier space}\label{app:interface}
This appendix gives the rows of the interface system \eqref{eq:interface_system}. For a plane mode
$\propto e^{i\bk\cdot\bx_\parallel+sz}$ with velocity amplitude $\hat\bu$ and pressure amplitude $\hat p$, the tractions
at $z=0$ in a medium of viscosity $\mu_j$ are
\begin{equation*}
  \hat\sigma_{xz}=\mu_j\left(s\hat u_x+ik_x\hat u_z\right),\quad
  \hat\sigma_{yz}=\mu_j\left(s\hat u_y+ik_y\hat u_z\right),\quad
  \hat\sigma_{zz}=-\hat p+2\mu_js\hat u_z .
\end{equation*}
The potential mode has $\hat\bu=(ik_x,ik_y,s)$ and $\hat p=c_j$, with $s=-k$ (reflected) or $s=k$ (transmitted). The
poloidal mode has $\hat\bu=(ik_xs,ik_ys,k^2)$ and the toroidal mode $\hat\bu=(ik_y,-ik_x,0)$, both with $\hat p=0$ and
with $s=-q$ (reflected) or $s=q_p$ (transmitted). With superscripts ``inc'', $R$ and $T$ for the incident, reflected and
transmitted fields, the six rows are
\begin{align*}
  &\hat\bu^{R}-\hat\bu^{T}=-\hat\bu^{\rm inc}\quad(3\ \text{rows}),\qquad
  \hat\sigma^{R}_{zz}-\hat\sigma^{T}_{zz}=-\hat\sigma^{\rm inc}_{zz},\\
  &\hat\sigma^{T}_{\tau z}-\hat\sigma^{R}_{\tau z}-\beta_p\alpha_p\hat u^{R}_\tau
   =\hat\sigma^{\rm inc}_{\tau z}+\beta_p\alpha_p\hat u^{\rm inc}_\tau\quad(\tau=x,y).
\end{align*}
The columns of $\mathsf A$ are normalised to unit Euclidean norm before solving, which removes the $O(k)$ degeneracy of
the mode vectors as $k\to0$. For a rigid wall the system reduces to $\hat\bu^R=-\hat\bu^{\rm inc}$.

\section{Convergence and admissibility checks}\label{app:checks}
This appendix collects the checks that support the accuracy and physical admissibility of the results. The scripts
are listed in the Data Availability Statement.

\emph{Energy identity for the homogeneous problem.} With $U=0$, the dot product of each momentum equation with
$\bar\bu$, integrated over its domain, gives
$\int(\nabla\bar\bu:\bsig+\tfrac{\mu}{k}|\bu|^2-i\omega\rho|\bu|^2)\,\dd V=\oint\bar\bu\cdot\bsig\cdot\bn\,\dd S$, with
$1/k=0$ in the fluid. Since $\nabla\cdot\bu=0$, the real part of $\nabla\bar\bu:\bsig$ is the viscous dissipation
$2\mu_j|\mathsf E|^2$, where $\mathsf E$ is the rate of strain, and the inertial term is purely imaginary. The surface
terms at infinity vanish. On the interfaces the normal velocity and the normal stress are continuous, so only
tangential terms remain. The stress jumps \eqref{eq:bc_shell} and \eqref{eq:bc_plane} contribute
$-\beta\mu/\sqrt k\int|\bu_\tau|^2\dd S$, and the core slip \eqref{eq:bc_core} contributes
$(\mu_s/\ell_c)\oint|\bm\Pi\cdot\bu|^2\dd S\ge0$. Collecting the real parts gives the identity of \cref{rem:energy}.

\emph{Quadrature.} Doubling the radial nodes ($N_k$) and the angular nodes ($N_\theta=8\to16$) changes the reflection
matrix by at most $9\times10^{-15}$ relative. The cases tested were: steady rigid wall ($\Wo=0.05$, $\varepsilon=1$,
normal); $\Wo=3$, $\varepsilon=1$, $\alpha_p=10$, parallel; $\Wo=30$, $\varepsilon=1$, $\alpha_p=20$, normal; $\Wo=3$,
$\varepsilon=0.25$, rigid wall; and $\Wo=10$, $\varepsilon=4$, $\alpha_p=50$, parallel. Crossovers located on a
120-point grid with doubled quadrature coincide with those of \cref{fig:woc_ap}.

\emph{Reflection series.} The spectral radius of $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM$ is largest for an impermeable sphere near a
rigid wall at low $\Wo$: $0.49$ at $\varepsilon=1$, $0.62$ at $\varepsilon=0.5$ and $0.69$ at $\varepsilon=0.25$. Over
the sampled particles, layers and frequencies it never exceeds $0.50$ at $\varepsilon=1$, so the Neumann series of
\cref{prop:series} converges throughout.

\emph{Dipolar truncation.} In the steady rigid-wall limit, the difference between the closure and the classical
reflection series $1/(1-\tfrac98x+\tfrac12x^3)$ is $1.8\times10^{-2}$, $1.4\times10^{-3}$ and $5.7\times10^{-5}$ at
$x=1/2$, $1/3$ and $1/5$. This equals $(0.29,\,0.11,\,0.04)\,x^4$, which is consistent with a first omitted term of order
$x^4$. For $\varepsilon\gtrsim9$ the comparison is limited by the Richardson extrapolation, whose error grows with
$\Wo\,h$.

\emph{Admissibility.} The wall-induced damping is positive in every stored spectrum (minimum $0.035$). The total
damping $-\operatorname{Re}(F/U)$ is positive for every configuration of \cref{sec:validation,sec:rigid,sec:soft},
including $\beta_p=0.5$ over $0.05\le\Wo\le250$ and $3\le\alpha_p\le100$ (minimum $1.26$) and the soft particle of
\cref{tab:sensitivity} with $\beta_s=0.5$ (minimum $1.08$). Negative stress jumps ($\beta_s,\beta_p=-1$) only add
dissipation, in agreement with \cref{rem:energy}. Positive ones can make an isolated porous particle non-dissipative:
for $\kappa=0$, $\alpha_s=5$ and $\beta_s=1$ the steady dissipation is negative, which motivates the admissible range
$\beta\le0.5$.
