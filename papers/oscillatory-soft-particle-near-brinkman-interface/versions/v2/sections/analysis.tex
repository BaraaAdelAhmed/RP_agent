\section{Solution Method}\label{sec:analysis}

The method solves the particle problem exactly in spherical harmonics, the layer problem exactly in Fourier space, and
couples the two through the dipolar moments of the incident flow. These moments are the only ones that reach the
force (\cref{lem:faxen}). All quantities in this section are dimensionless, so that $b=1$, and we abbreviate
$\lambda\equiv\lambda_fb$.

\subsection{Exact response of the core--shell particle}\label{sec:sphere}
In each domain the velocity splits into a rigid part, a potential part and a vortical part, which lets every boundary
condition be expressed through scalar radial functions.

\begin{lemma}[Potential--vortical decomposition]\label{lem:decomp}
In domain $j$, $\bu=\bu_{\rm rig}+\nabla\Phi+\bm w$, with $\nabla^2\Phi=0$, $\nabla\cdot\bm w=0$ and
$(\nabla^2-\lambda_j^2)\bm w=\bm 0$. Here $\bu_{\rm rig}=\be$ in the shell and $\bm 0$ elsewhere. The vortical part
carries no pressure, and the pressures are
\begin{equation}
  p^{(1)}=c_1\Phi^{(1)},\qquad p^{(2)}=i\Wo^2\,\be\cdot\bx+c_2\Phi^{(2)},\qquad p^{(3)}=c_3\Phi^{(3)},
  \label{eq:pressures}
\end{equation}
with pressure coefficients $c_1=i\Wo^2$, $c_2=i\Wo^2-\alpha_s^2$ and $c_3=i\Wo^2-\alpha_p^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For $\bu=\nabla\Phi$ with $\Phi$ harmonic, the viscous term of \cref{eq:governing} vanishes and the remaining terms are
gradients, which gives \cref{eq:pressures}; the rigid motion of the shell contributes the gradient of
$i\Wo^2\be\cdot\bx$. For $\bm w$ the momentum equation reduces to $\mu_j(\nabla^2-\lambda_j^2)\bm w=\nabla p$, which
holds with zero pressure by \cref{eq:lambdas}. Conversely, the divergence of each momentum equation gives
$\nabla^2p=0$. Since $c_j\neq0$ whenever $\Wo>0$, $\Phi=(p-p_{\rm rig})/c_j$ is harmonic, and
$\bm w=\bu-\bu_{\rm rig}-\nabla\Phi$ then satisfies the homogeneous vortical equation.
\end{proof}

The radial functions are the decaying and regular solutions of $(\nabla^2-\lambda_j^2)g=0$,
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal K_j(r)=\frac{e^{-\lambda_j r}}{r},\qquad \mathcal I_j(r)=\frac{\sinh\lambda_j r}{r},\qquad j\in\{f,s\},
\end{equation*}
which are modified spherical Bessel functions of order zero. Because the particle is spherically symmetric, its response
preserves each spherical-harmonic sector about $\bx_0$, and the following lemma restricts the problem to the dipolar
($l=1$) sector.

\begin{lemma}[Force from the centre values of the incident flow]\label{lem:faxen}
Let $\bu_{\rm inc}=\nabla\Phi_{\rm inc}+\bm w_{\rm inc}$ satisfy \cref{eq:stokes} and be regular in a ball containing the
particle. The force on the particle then depends on $\bu_{\rm inc}$ only through the centre values
\begin{equation}
  U_p=\be\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\rm inc}(\bx_0),\qquad w_0=\be\cdot\bm w_{\rm inc}(\bx_0).
  \label{eq:centre_values}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Expanded about $\bx_0$, $\Phi_{\rm inc}$ is a sum of solid harmonics $r^lY_l^m$, and $\bm w_{\rm inc}$ a sum of regular
poloidal and toroidal fields built on $i_l(\lambda r)Y_l^m$. The net force is the dipolar moment of the surface
traction, and the particle maps each sector onto itself, so only the $l=1$ poloidal sector contributes. The $l=1$
toroidal field is a rigid rotation, which produces a torque but no force. Every regular field of order $l\ge2$ vanishes
at $\bx_0$, because its velocity is $O(r^{l-1})$. Since $\nabla^2\nabla\Phi=\bm 0$ whereas
$\nabla^2\bm w=\lambda^2\bm w$, the potential and vortical $l=1$ amplitudes are independent and are fixed by
\cref{eq:centre_values}.
\end{proof}

Every dipolar field directed along $\be$ is a combination of a uniform flow, the potential dipole
$\nabla(\be\cdot\nabla r^{-1})$, and the vortical fields $\curl\curl(\be g)=\nabla(\be\cdot\nabla g)-\lambda_j^2\be g$
with $g=\mathcal K_j$ or $\mathcal I_j$. With $\cos\theta=\be\cdot(\bx-\bx_0)/r$, a vortical field has the components
\begin{equation}
  u_r=\left(g''-\lambda_j^2g\right)\cos\theta,\qquad u_\theta=\left(\lambda_j^2g-\frac{g'}{r}\right)\sin\theta,
  \label{eq:vort_radial}
\end{equation}
while the potential dipole has $u_r=2r^{-3}\cos\theta$ and $u_\theta=r^{-3}\sin\theta$. The fields in the fluid and in
the shell are therefore
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:fields}
\begin{align}
  \bu^{(1)}&=U_p\,\be+D\,\curl\curl(\be\,\mathcal I_f)
  +B\,\nabla\!\left(\be\cdot\nabla\tfrac1r\right)+C\,\curl\curl(\be\,\mathcal K_f),
  \label{eq:field_ext}\\
  \bu^{(2)}&=\be+E_1\be+E_2\,\nabla\!\left(\be\cdot\nabla\tfrac1r\right)
  +G_1\,\curl\curl(\be\,\mathcal I_s)+G_2\,\curl\curl(\be\,\mathcal K_s).
  \label{eq:field_shell}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The first two terms of \cref{eq:field_ext} are the flow incident from the layer. Their amplitudes follow from the centre
values \eqref{eq:centre_values}, because $\mathcal I_f(r)=\lambda+\lambda^3r^2/6+O(r^4)$ gives $w_0=-\tfrac23\lambda^3D$.
The coefficients $B$ and $C$ measure the potential and vortical dipoles scattered by the particle. Writing
$u_r=\mathcal U(r)\cos\theta$, $u_\theta=\mathcal V(r)\sin\theta$ and $p=\mathcal P(r)\cos\theta$ gives the traction
amplitudes
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal S_{rr}=-\mathcal P+2\mu_j\,\mathcal U',\qquad
  \mathcal S_{r\theta}=\mu_j\Bigl(\mathcal V'-\frac{\mathcal V}{r}-\frac{\mathcal U}{r}\Bigr),
\end{equation*}
in which $\mu_j$ is written in units of $\mu$. Integrating the traction over the sphere yields the force
\begin{equation}
  F=\frac{4\pi}{3}\left[\mathcal S^{(1)}_{rr}(1)-2\,\mathcal S^{(1)}_{r\theta}(1)\right].
  \label{eq:force_amplitudes}
\end{equation}

The conditions \eqref{eq:bc_core} at $r=\kappa$ and \eqref{eq:bc_shell} at $r=1$ give six linear equations for the
coefficients $B$, $C$, $E_1$, $E_2$, $G_1$ and $G_2$, driven by $U$, $U_p$ and $w_0$ (\cref{app:sphere}). For $\kappa=0$, regularity at the centre
removes $E_2$, $G_2$ and the core conditions; for $\kappa=1$ the two slip conditions on $r=1$ determine $B$ and $C$.
Linearity and \cref{lem:faxen} then give the response of the particle to its own motion and to any incident flow,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}B\\ C\end{pmatrix}=\bm s_U\,U+\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\begin{pmatrix}U_p\\ w_0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  F=Z_U\,U+\bm z_{\rm inc}^{\mathsf T}\begin{pmatrix}U_p\\ w_0\end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
where the vectors $\bm s_U$, $\bm z_{\rm inc}=(Z_p,Z_w)^{\mathsf T}$ and the matrix $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}$ depend only on
$\Wo$ and the particle parameters $(\kappa,\alpha_s,\beta_s,\hat\ell_c,\mu_s/\mu)$. In particular, $F_\infty=Z_UU$. For
an impermeable no-slip particle these quantities take closed forms, which anchor the validation in
\cref{sec:validation}.

\begin{proposition}[Impermeable no-slip particle]\label{prop:rigid}
For $\kappa=1$ and $\hat\ell_c=0$,
\begin{equation}
  B=\frac{3+3\lambda+\lambda^2}{2\lambda^2}\,U,\qquad C=-\frac{3e^{\lambda}}{2\lambda^2}\,U,\qquad
  Z_U=-6\pi\Bigl(1+\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{9}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:rigid_BC}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  Z_p=6\pi\Bigl(1+\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{3}\Bigr),\qquad Z_w=6\pi e^{\lambda}.
  \label{eq:rigid_Z}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Imposing $u_r=\cos\theta$ and $u_\theta=-\sin\theta$ at $r=1$ on \cref{eq:field_ext} with $U_p=w_0=0$ gives two linear
equations, whose solution is the pair $(B,C)$ of \cref{eq:rigid_BC}; substituting it into \cref{eq:force_amplitudes}
gives $Z_U$. The coefficients \eqref{eq:rigid_Z} follow from the generalised Faxén theorem of Mazur and
Bedeaux~\cite{mazur1974generalization}, $F=6\pi[(1+\lambda)\langle\bu_{\rm inc}\rangle_S+\tfrac13\lambda^2\langle\bu_{\rm
inc}\rangle_V]$, where the brackets are surface and volume averages over the particle. A uniform flow has both averages
equal to $U_p$. Each Cartesian component of $\bm w_{\rm inc}$ obeys the modified Helmholtz equation, so the mean-value
theorem gives $\langle w\rangle_S=w_0\sinh\lambda/\lambda$ and $\langle w\rangle_V=3w_0(\lambda\cosh\lambda-\sinh\lambda)/\lambda^3$,
whose combination is $Z_w=6\pi(\cosh\lambda+\sinh\lambda)$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:rigid} recovers both classical limits. As $\lambda\to0$, the $O(\lambda^{-2})$ parts of $B$ and $C$ combine
into the Stokeslet, and $B+C\to-\tfrac14$ is the dipole of Stokes' solution. As $|\lambda|\to\infty$, $B\to\tfrac12$
gives the dipole of inviscid flow, while the vortical field $C\,\mathcal K_f\propto e^{-\lambda(r-1)}$ is confined to a
boundary layer of thickness $O(|\lambda|^{-1})$ around the particle. The term $\tfrac13\lambda^2$ in $Z_p$ contains the
Froude--Krylov force $6\pi\cdot\tfrac29\lambda^2$, which is exerted by the pressure gradient that accelerates the incident
flow.

\subsection{Fourier reflection at the Brinkman interface}\label{sec:reflection}
The scattered dipoles $(B,C)$ drive the flow that the layer reflects back onto the particle. With the two-dimensional
transform $f(\bx)=\int\hat f(\bk,z)\,e^{i\bk\cdot\bx_\parallel}\,\dd^2k/(2\pi)^2$, wavenumber $k=|\bk|$ and decay rates
$q=\sqrt{k^2+\lambda^2}$ and $q_p=\sqrt{k^2+\lambda_p^2}$ (principal branches), the Weyl-type identities
$\widehat{1/r}=(2\pi/k)\,e^{k(z-h)}$ and $\widehat{\mathcal K_f}=(2\pi/q)\,e^{q(z-h)}$, valid for $z<h$, turn the
scattered field into plane modes travelling towards the layer:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:incident_fourier}
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\nabla\Phi}_{\rm sc}&=B\,\bm g_k\,(\bm g_k\cdot\be)\,\frac{2\pi}{k}\,e^{k(z-h)},\\
  \widehat{\bm w}_{\rm sc}&=C\left[\bm g_q(\bm g_q\cdot\be)-\lambda^2\be\right]\frac{2\pi}{q}\,e^{q(z-h)},
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with $\bm g_k=(ik_x,ik_y,k)$ and $\bm g_q=(ik_x,ik_y,q)$. The reflected field in $z>0$ is built, by \cref{lem:decomp},
from a potential mode $\nabla e^{-kz}$, a poloidal mode $\curl\curl(\be_ze^{-qz})$ and a toroidal mode
$\curl(\be_ze^{-qz})$. The transmitted field in $z<0$ is built from $\nabla e^{kz}$, $\curl\curl(\be_ze^{q_pz})$ and
$\curl(\be_ze^{q_pz})$; all modes carry the factor $e^{i\bk\cdot\bx_\parallel}$. The six interface conditions
\eqref{eq:bc_plane} fix the reflected amplitudes $(R_\Phi,R_{\rm pol},R_{\rm tor})$ and transmitted amplitudes
$(T_\Phi,T_{\rm pol},T_{\rm tor})$ at every wavevector through a $6\times6$ system
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf A(\bk;\Wo,\alpha_p,\beta_p,\mu_p/\mu)\,\bm x(\bk)=\bm r(\bk),
  \label{eq:interface_system}
\end{equation}
whose right-hand side contains the velocity and traction of \cref{eq:incident_fourier} at $z=0$ (\cref{app:interface}).
Let $R_\Phi^{(n)}$, $R_{\rm pol}^{(n)}$ and $R_{\rm tor}^{(n)}$ denote the reflected amplitudes for a unit source of
type $n$, with $n=1$ for the potential dipole ($B=1$, $C=0$) and $n=2$ for the vortical dipole ($B=0$, $C=1$).
Projecting the reflected potential and vortical velocities at $\bx_0$ on $\be$ gives the centre values of the incident
flow in terms of the scattered dipoles,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}U_p\\ w_0\end{pmatrix}=\bM\begin{pmatrix}B\\ C\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  \bM=\int\!\frac{\dd^2k}{(2\pi)^2}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \be\cdot\bm g_k^-\,R^{(1)}_\Phi\,e^{-kh} & \be\cdot\bm g_k^-\,R^{(2)}_\Phi\,e^{-kh}\\[2pt]
    \be\cdot\bm v^{(1)}\,e^{-qh} & \be\cdot\bm v^{(2)}\,e^{-qh}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:reflection_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $\bm v^{(n)}=R^{(n)}_{\rm pol}\bm p_q^-+R^{(n)}_{\rm tor}\bm t$ is the reflected vortical velocity, with
$\bm g_k^-=(ik_x,ik_y,-k)$, $\bm p_q^-=(-iqk_x,-iqk_y,k^2)$ and $\bm t=(ik_y,-ik_x,0)$. The reflection matrix depends on
the layer, the height and the frequency but not on the particle, so that one matrix serves every particle in a
parametric sweep.

\begin{proposition}[Transparent and impermeable limits]\label{prop:limits}
(i) If $\mu_p=\mu$, $\beta_p=0$ and $\alpha_p=0$, then $\bM=\mathsf 0$ and $F_{\rm int}=0$. (ii) As $\alpha_p\to\infty$,
$\bM$ tends to the reflection matrix of a rigid no-slip wall, obtained from $\hat\bu^{(1)}(\bk,0)=\bm 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) The transmitted modes then coincide with the incident ones and the traction jump vanishes, so zero reflection
satisfies \cref{eq:interface_system}, and the solution is unique. (ii) As $\alpha_p\to\infty$, $q_p\sim\alpha_p$ and
$c_3\sim-\alpha_p^2$ diverge, so bounded tractions require transmitted velocities of order $\alpha_p^{-1}$, and the
velocity conditions reduce to no slip, as anticipated in \cref{rem:bj}.
\end{proof}

For $\be=\be_z$ or $\be_x$ the integrand of \cref{eq:reflection_matrix} is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most
two in the polar angle of $\bk$. The angular integral is therefore exact with $N_\theta=8$ equally spaced nodes. The
radial integral uses composite 64-point Gauss--Legendre panels on $0\le kh\le50$, with breaks at $k\sim|\lambda|$ and
$k\sim|\lambda_p|$ and geometric grading towards $k=0$, so that $N_k\approx2000$; the integrand decays at least as
$e^{-2kh}$. Doubling either resolution changes $\bM$ by less than $10^{-13}$ relative.

\subsection{Self-consistent dipolar closure and limiting regimes}\label{sec:closure}
Substituting the reflection \eqref{eq:reflection_matrix} into the particle response \eqref{eq:response} closes the
problem in the dipolar sector:
\begin{equation}
  \left(\mathsf I-\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM\right)\begin{pmatrix}B\\ C\end{pmatrix}=\bm s_U\,U,
  \qquad
  F_{\rm int}=\bm z_{\rm inc}^{\mathsf T}\bM\left(\mathsf I-\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM\right)^{-1}\bm s_U\,U .
  \label{eq:closure}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Summation of reflections]\label{prop:series}
If the spectral radius of $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM$ is below one, \cref{eq:closure} equals the series
$\sum_{n\ge0}\bm z_{\rm inc}^{\mathsf T}\bM(\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM)^n\bm s_U\,U$, whose $n$th term is the $(n+1)$th
reflection of the method of reflections~\cite{simha2018unsteady} restricted to the dipolar sector.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand $(\mathsf I-\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM)^{-1}$ as a Neumann series. Each factor $\bM$ reflects the current dipoles at
the layer, and each factor $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}$ is the dipolar response of the particle to the reflected flow
(\cref{lem:faxen}).
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of \cref{prop:series} holds throughout this work: the spectral radius of $\mathsf S_{\rm inc}\bM$ does not
exceed $0.50$ at $\varepsilon=1$ and is $0.69$ even at $\varepsilon=0.25$ (\cref{app:checks}). The proposition also
identifies the approximation: the closure keeps every order of reflection but discards the $l\ge2$ parts of the
reflected flow. These parts exert no force directly, but the particle's response to them would be reflected again. For
an impermeable sphere near a rigid wall in the steady limit, the closure agrees with the classical reflection series
through $O(x^3)$, $x=b/h$, and differs from it by terms of size $O(x^4)$; the resulting error is quantified against the
exact solution in \cref{sec:validation} and \cref{app:checks}. The complete procedure is summarised in
\cref{alg:force}. One evaluation of $\bM$ requires about $1.6\times10^4$ solutions of the $6\times6$ system
\eqref{eq:interface_system}, which takes about $0.05$\,s vectorised on one laptop core, while the particle response costs
$O(1)$. Crossover numbers are found by bracketing $\hat\chi_{\rm int}$ on a logarithmic grid of 70 values of $\Wo$ and
refining each root with Brent's method.

Two limits of the closure have closed forms. At high frequency the reflected vorticity is exponentially small, so only
the potential image of the particle acts back on it, and the added mass reduces to its inviscid value.

\begin{proposition}[Inviscid limit near a rigid wall]\label{prop:inviscid}
For an impermeable no-slip particle near a rigid wall, as $\Wo\to\infty$ at fixed $h$, the first reflection gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{m_{\rm int}}{m_f}\to\frac{3}{16}\frac{b^3}{h^3}\ \ (\be=\be_z),\qquad
  \frac{m_{\rm int}}{m_f}\to\frac{3}{32}\frac{b^3}{h^3}\ \ (\be=\be_x).
  \label{eq:inviscid}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
As $|\lambda|\to\infty$, $B\to\tfrac12$, $Z_p\to2\pi\lambda^2$, and the vortical scattered and reflected fields are
confined to layers of thickness $O(|\lambda|^{-1})$ around the particle and the wall, so their contribution at
$\bx_0$ is exponentially small in $|\lambda|h$. The potential dipole is reflected by the no-penetration condition into a
mirror dipole at $-h\be_z$, of opposite sign for $\be_z$ and equal sign for $\be_x$. At distance $2h$ it induces
$U_p=-B/(4h^3)$ and $U_p=-B/(8h^3)$, respectively. With $F_{\rm int}=Z_pU_p$, $\lambda^2=-i\Wo^2$ and
\cref{eq:observables}, this gives \cref{eq:inviscid}. These are the leading image corrections
$\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac38(b/h)^3$ and $\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac3{16}(b/h)^3$ to the inviscid added-mass coefficient
$\tfrac12$.
\end{proof}

At low frequency the closure reduces to the steady problem of a slip or porous particle near a Brinkman interface with
stress jump, studied for the normal motion of a slip sphere in Ref.~\onlinecite{faltas2021axisymmetric}. Direct
evaluation at $\Wo\lesssim10^{-3}$ loses precision through the cancellation of the $O(\lambda^{-2})$ terms of $B$ and $C$.
We therefore obtain the steady values by Richardson extrapolation from $\Wo=0.01$ and $0.02$, which removes the leading
$O(\lambda)$ unsteady correction. Finally, \cref{eq:lambdas_nd} shows that $\lambda_p\to\lambda_f$ when $\Wo\gg\alpha_p$
and $\mu_p=\mu$. By \cref{prop:limits}(i), a layer with a small stress jump then becomes hydrodynamically transparent,
which is the origin of the high-frequency regime analysed in \cref{sec:rigid}.
