{+\section{Rigid Particle near a Permeable Layer: Spectra, Crossovers and Transparency}\label{sec:rigid}+}

{+We first isolate the effect of the layer by keeping the particle impermeable ($\kappa=1$). This section follows the+}
{+interaction force from the steady regime to the inertial regime, and then condenses the spectra into a map of the+}
{+crossover Womersley numbers, which is the quantity accessible to AFM and acoustic measurements.+}

{+\subsection{Interaction spectra from the viscous to the inertial regime}\label{sec:spectra}+}

{+\begin{figure*}[t]+}
{+  \centering+}
{+  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/fig3_spectra_alphap.pdf}+}
{+  \caption{Interaction spectra of an impermeable sphere at $\varepsilon=1$ and $\beta_p=0$, for a rigid wall and for+}
{+  layers with $\alpha_p=50,20,10,5$. Top: wall-induced damping $\hat\gamma_{\rm int}$ against $\Wo$. Bottom: reactive+}
{+  coefficient $\hat\chi_{\rm int}=\omega m_{\rm int}/(6\pi\mu b)$ against $\Wo$, on a symmetric logarithmic axis that is+}
{+  linear for $|\hat\chi_{\rm int}|<10^{-2}$; its sign is that of $m_{\rm int}$. (a) Normal, (b) parallel oscillation.}+}
{+  \label{fig:spectra}+}
{+\end{figure*}+}

{+\textbf{Near a rigid wall, the wall-induced added mass is negative at low frequency and positive at high frequency.} In+}
{+\cref{fig:spectra} (blue curves), $\hat\chi_{\rm int}$ changes sign at $\Wo_c=2.92$ in normal and at $0.955$ in parallel+}
{+oscillation. Above the crossover the added mass approaches the image value of \cref{prop:inviscid}. Below it the sign+}
{+is opposite to that of any inviscid image, which points to a viscous origin. In an unbounded fluid, the term+}
{+$-6\pi\lambda$ of $Z_U$ is the frequency-domain form of the Basset history force~\cite{maxey1983equation}. Its reactive+}
{+part $6\pi\Wo/\sqrt2$ is carried by vorticity that diffuses over the distance $|\lambda_f|^{-1}$ during a period. When+}
{+$|\lambda_f|^{-1}$ exceeds the gap, this vorticity reaches the wall and is annihilated there. The computed spectra+}
{+confirm this quantitatively: as $\Wo\to0$, $-\hat\chi_{\rm int}$ tends to the full unbounded Basset term $\Wo/\sqrt2$.+}
{+The ratio $-\hat\chi_{\rm int}/(\Wo/\sqrt2)$ equals $0.99$ (normal) and $0.96$ (parallel) at $\Wo=0.01$ and+}
{+$\varepsilon=1$, and it decreases as $\delta_v$ becomes smaller than the gap. \finding{The negative added mass at low+}
{+$\Wo$ is therefore the part of the Basset memory force that the wall removes. It is the+}
{+frequency-domain counterpart of the wall-induced change in the long-time velocity tail of a Brownian+}
{+sphere~\cite{felderhof2005effect}, which has been observed with optical tweezers~\cite{mo2015broadband}.} With+}
{+\cref{rem:sign}, the sign change reproduces qualitatively the crossover of the AFM frequency shift reported by Zhang+}
{+\emph{et al.}~\cite{zhang2023unsteady}. For a probe of radius $b=10\,\mu$m in water, $\Wo_c=2.92$ corresponds to+}
{+$f\approx14$\,kHz, which lies in the range of colloidal-probe AFM.+}

{+The damping follows a different pattern. Near the rigid wall, $\hat\gamma_{\rm int}$ falls from its steady value $0.98$+}
{+to a minimum of $0.31$ at $\Wo\approx3.7$ and then rises, because the Stokes layer on the wall, whose thickness scales+}
{+as $\delta_v$, dissipates more energy as it thins.+}

{+\textbf{A permeable layer weakens the steady interaction and introduces a dissipation maximum at+}
{+$\Wo=O(\alpha_p)$.} In the steady limit, fluid escapes through the interface, so that $\hat\gamma_{\rm int}(\Wo\to0)$+}
{+decreases from $0.98$ for the rigid wall to $0.94$, $0.89$ and $0.78$ for $\alpha_p=20$, $10$ and $5$ (normal). At high+}
{+frequency the damping departs from the rigid-wall curve and passes through a maximum. On an extended frequency range+}
{+the maximum lies at $\Wo=9.8$, $29.6$ and $105$ for $\alpha_p=10$, $20$ and $50$, i.e.\ at $\Wo/\alpha_p=1.0$--$2.1$.+}
{+For $\alpha_p\ge20$ it exceeds the rigid-wall damping at the same frequency, by a factor $2.2$ for $\alpha_p=50$+}
{+($\hat\gamma_{\rm int}=6.7$ against $3.1$). \finding{The maximum appears where the viscous penetration depth becomes+}
{+comparable with the Brinkman screening length $\sqrt{k_p\mu_p/\mu}$ of the layer, so that the oscillatory flow enters the+}
{+layer and does work against its Darcy drag. This dissipation channel is absent at a rigid wall, and it also governs the+}
{+unsteady flow next to an oscillating porous wall~\cite{sherwood2020unsteady}.} We state this mechanism as an+}
{+interpretation consistent with the location of the maximum, not as a derived result. For $\alpha_p=5$, $\hat\chi_{\rm int}$ remains negative at every frequency, so the+}
{+positive added mass found near a rigid wall is absent; the next subsection explains why.+}

{+\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}+}
{+The dipolar closure is accurate to $5\%$ in normal and $1.5\%$ in parallel oscillation at $\varepsilon=1$, and to better+}
{+than $1\%$ for $\varepsilon\ge2$ (\cref{sec:validation}). It does not resolve lubrication, so the near-contact regime+}
{+$\varepsilon<1$, relevant to probes in contact with a brush, requires higher multipoles. We expect the crossover locations+}
{+to be less sensitive to this error than the force magnitudes, because the neglected $l\ge2$ terms mainly rescale the+}
{+interaction. \TODO{authors: confirm with a multipole or finite-element computation at $\varepsilon=1$.} The model also+}
{+assumes rigid porous matrices, a layer thick compared with $h$ (\cref{as:thick}), a non-rotating particle+}
{+(\cref{as:norotation}) and Brinkman viscosities equal to $\mu$. Poroelastic deformation of the glycocalyx~\cite{batalha2026elasto} and a finite layer thickness are expected to+}
{+modify mainly the high-frequency branch, where the flow penetrates the layer.+}
