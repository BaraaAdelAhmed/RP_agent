# 2nd draft paper

You act first as a severe senior referee for a top applied-mathematics / fluid-mechanics journal, then as the
corresponding author who answers that referee by improving the paper. The referee's job is to reach a solid verdict
on whether the method is sharp and the physical findings are sound; the author's job is to make the paper's strongest
points impossible to miss.

Read first, and follow throughout: `docs/paper-standards.md` (mathematics, typesetting, figure validity and quality,
the schematic, and §6 the author's house writing style). Every new or changed sentence obeys §6; every new or changed
figure passes §3–§4.

## Tools

All `python3 scripts/rp.py` commands of the other paper skills (`status`, `search`, `addref`, `rmref`, `check`,
`compile`, `symbols`, `lint`, `layout`, `render`, `diff`), plus:

| Command | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `rp.py snapshot <slug>` | Copy all sources to `versions/vN/` before editing (never edit `versions/`) |
| `rp.py figures <slug>` | Every figure: label, files, panel count, caption, where cited (file:line, section), the script that makes it |
| `rp.py cites <slug>` | Every reference: each place it is cited with the sentence; uncited entries; cited keys missing from the .bib |

`references.bib` changes only through `addref` / `rmref`. Commit `papers/<slug>/` (with `.session/`, `versions/`,
`notes/`, `code/`) and push to the session branch at the end of every stage.

Decisions: you do not stop for approval in this skill. When the review finds a serious flaw, fix it (verify the fix
independently), record it in the report, and continue. Change the title directly. Report every such decision clearly
at the end.

## Stage 0 — Set up
`rp.py status <slug>`; read `notes/novelty_report.md`, `notes/key_findings.md`, `notes/figure_review.md` and
`notes/draft1_report.md` if present; `rp.py snapshot <slug>`.

## Stage 1 — Referee review → `notes/referee_report.md`
Review as a demanding referee who wants to find what is wrong, not to confirm what is written. Write the report with,
for each issue, the location, the evidence and a severity (**fatal**, **major**, **minor**):

1. **Method sharpness.**
   - Re-derive the central results independently of the paper's code: a different route (an asymptotic limit,
     another formulation or numerical method, a symbolic derivation with sympy). Write the checks as scripts under
     `notes/checks/` and keep their outputs.
   - Convergence study of every truncation and discretisation (series order, quadrature points, reflection
     iterations, mesh), with the observed convergence rate and a stated tolerance; an error budget for the plotted
     quantities.
   - Recovery of every classical limit the paper claims, with numbers.
2. **Physical soundness of every finding.** For each finding in `notes/key_findings.md` and each figure: the expected
   behaviour from physics alone (limits, signs, bounds, scalings, symmetries, energy/dissipation positivity,
   causality relations where they apply), the literature that confirms or contradicts it, and the verdict. A finding
   that cannot be explained by a stated mechanism, or that contradicts established physics without overwhelming
   evidence, is not sound.
3. **Realistic parameters.** Map every dimensionless group and range to real systems with literature values (cite
   them), state which physical situations each range represents, and check that the model's assumptions hold there
   (for example small amplitude so that convective inertia is negligible, the validity range of the porous-medium
   model, linear response). Flag results shown only in unphysical ranges.
4. **Scoop check.** Rerun the core searches of the novelty report (`rp.py search` with `--year-from` the year of the
   report, plus web search) for work published since; compare and say whether the novelty claim still holds and
   which new papers must be cited and distinguished.
5. **Number consistency.** Every number in the abstract, text, tables, captions and conclusion is traced to the script
   output that produced it; list mismatches.
6. **Reproducibility.** Every figure and table is regenerated by a script in `code/` or `figures/` with parameters
   matching the paper (`rp.py figures` shows the scripts); list anything that cannot be regenerated.
7. **Verdict:** accept / minor revision / major revision / reject, with the reasons.

**Then fix.** Correct every fatal and major issue: fix computations, rerun and regenerate the affected figures and
tables, re-verify independently, and update the text. A result that cannot be made sound is removed or reframed
honestly. Add a "Response" column to the report: what was done for each issue. The review is complete only when the
re-run of items 1–6 gives a verdict of accept or minor revision; say so explicitly in the report.

## Stage 2 — The 5 strongest points → `notes/strongest_points.md`
Read the whole manuscript and extract the 5 points that most distinguish this work. For each: one precise sentence;
the evidence in the paper (equation, figure, table); why it is new compared with the closest prior work (cited);
and why it matters physically or practically. Rank them. Points must survive the referee review; never inflate.

## Stage 3 — Placing the strongest points
- **Abstract** and **Conclusion** (bullet points where the paper uses them): each strongest point appears explicitly,
  with its quantitative content where it has one.
- **Body:** find the places where each point is established (where its figure, equation or table is discussed) and
  state it there clearly, in prose that follows §6; make sure the Introduction guide points list them as the
  contributions. Each point should be visible to a reader who reads only the abstract, the figure captions and the
  conclusion.
- **Title:** rewrite it so it conveys the sense of the strongest points (above all the first) while keeping the
  structure of a research-paper title (the system, the physics, the key effect) and as few words as possible (aim for
  at most 15). Keep the previous title and your reasoning in the report, and put it in the comment block of
  candidate titles in `main.tex`.

## Stage 4 — Figures → `notes/figure_plan.md`
1. **Inventory** (`rp.py figures`): for every figure, list the parameters it varies and holds fixed, the quantity
   plotted, the claim it supports, and its weaknesses (redundant with another figure, too few curves, missing regime,
   no comparison).
2. **Gaps:** from the inventory and the strongest points, decide which new figures would strengthen the paper. Always
   consider:
   - **Streamlines** (or velocity / vorticity fields) with **at least 4 panels**, chosen to show the physical contrast
     behind the main findings (for example rigid vs permeable wall, in-phase vs out-of-phase parts of the flow,
     low vs high frequency, impermeable vs soft particle). Compute the fields from the paper's solution, not a
     separate model; use the same non-dimensionalisation; mark the particle and interface geometry; equal aspect
     ratio; consistent colour scale across panels.
   - **Error analysis**, when the numerical method truncates or discretises: convergence of the key quantities with
     truncation order or resolution, on log axes, with the observed rate and the tolerance.
   - **Regime map:** the parameter plane divided into the regions of each behaviour (for example where a crossover
     exists, where the layer is transparent).
   - **Asymptotic vs exact overlays** wherever the paper derives limits.
   - **Comparison with published data** (experiments or numerics from the literature, cited), where such data exist.
   - Any other figure that a referee would expect for the strongest points.
3. **Budget:** merge or remove weak or redundant figures so the set stays focused; justify each change.
4. **Make them:** write each figure's script under `code/` (or `figures/`), generate it to the quality rules of
   §4 (dense sampling, legends outside crowded axes, print size), render it (`rp.py render --figures`) and look at it,
   and pass it through the physics review of §3 into `notes/figure_review.md` before writing any discussion. Then
   integrate it: caption, first citation before the float, and discussion in flowing prose per §6.

## Stage 5 — References
1. Map citations (`rp.py cites`). For each citation, check that the cited work supports that exact sentence (read
   its abstract with `rp.py search` on its title, or web fetch); move citations to the sentence they support, or to
   the section where the reader needs them (background in the Introduction guide points, models in the Problem
   Formulation, methods in the Analysis, comparisons in the Results); remove padding citations.
2. Add well-established references (seminal papers, monographs, major reviews; confirmed to exist, through
   `addref`) for every unusual physical term, model or finding that a reader might question (for example a
   stress-jump condition, a Basset-type memory force, a particular porous-medium model), and recent work (last 2–3
   years), including anything the scoop check found.
3. Citation wording and consistency per §6 ("studied by Faltas et al. [12]"); uncited .bib entries either cited where
   they belong or removed; every entry has a DOI or arXiv ID.

## Stage 6 — Mathematics to appendices
Read the body as a reader who wants the problem's flow: every mathematical block that is not essential to following
the argument (long derivations, coefficient lists, matrix entries, auxiliary identities, convergence proofs, special
cases) moves to an appendix, written there in full with every step, and the body keeps the statement, the numbered
result and its meaning with a pointer to the appendix. Check that the appendices are ordered as the body refers to
them and that each opens with a sentence saying what it establishes.

## Stage 7 — Verify and hand over
1. `rp.py compile` (or `check`), `rp.py layout`, `rp.py lint` (all "House style" lists empty or justified),
   `rp.py symbols` (every symbol defined at first appearance), `rp.py diff`.
2. Re-check number consistency after all changes.
3. Write `notes/draft2_report.md`: the referee verdict before and after, every issue with its response, the 5
   strongest points and where each now appears, the old and new title, figures added/merged/removed, reference
   changes, mathematics moved to appendices, and everything the authors should still check.
4. Commit and push. Tell the author what to open first: `notes/draft2_report.md`, then the PDF.
